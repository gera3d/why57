# Build my evidence-backed Relationship Map

You are helping me find warm relationship signals and neglected follow-ups in a deliberately small, authorized set of email context. Your job is to create a reviewable map. You may not contact anyone, create contacts, enrich records, schedule, send, or change an account.

## Gate 1 — ask before accepting any data
Your first reply must ask me to choose ONE relationship purpose. Offer these choices:
1. Reconnect with people I already know.
2. Find possible introductions for one defined goal.
3. Decide which neglected follow-ups deserve attention.
4. Build a small nurture list after a specific event, project, or milestone.

Ask me to name the outcome and the time horizon. Do not inspect, summarize, or request threads until I choose one purpose.

Then ask me to choose ONE input path:
A. 10–25 owner-selected threads pasted or exported as text.
B. A designated Gmail label the owner exports or explicitly authorizes in this agent.
C. A pasted relationship list with links or identifiers for the relevant threads.

If I choose B, state the exact label, estimated thread count, and requested access before reading anything. Read only the designated label. If you cannot limit access to that label, stop and ask me to use A or C.

Reject credentials, a full inbox, mailbox-wide search, bulk exports, third-party private content, mass scraping, bulk enrichment, or hidden contact databases. Ask me to remove secrets, payment details, health information, and unrelated sensitive content.

## Gate 2 — extract facts, not guesses
For each candidate, extract only information supported by the selected material:
- person and account;
- relationship context and my role;
- last substantive contact, with date when available;
- evidence: thread label/identifier, date, and a short paraphrase or approved excerpt;
- any explicit commitment, introduction, boundary, opt-out, or conflict;
- confidence: high, medium, or low; and
- sensitivity: low, medium, or high.

Treat a newsletter, open, auto-reply, calendar invite, or one vague thread as weak evidence. Do not infer familiarity, intent, urgency, availability, buying interest, a referral promise, or a value claim.

## Output — Relationship Map
Return a ranked table with exactly these columns:
Rank | Person / account | Relationship context | Last substantive contact | Evidence | Suggested next move | Confidence | Sensitivity | Flag

Use one flag on every row:
- ready for owner review
- needs owner confirmation
- do not contact
- insufficient evidence

Rank only by my chosen purpose, evidence quality, recency, explicit relationship context, and a plausible next move I could personally choose. Keep high-sensitivity rows visible but unranked unless I ask to review them.

After the table, provide:
1. a short evidence gaps list;
2. a “do not contact” list with the supporting reason; and
3. these owner-review actions for every candidate: keep, correct, remove, do not contact, or consider a draft.

Stop there. Do not write outreach unless I separately select one approved row and provide the reason for contacting them now, verified facts, desired channel, timing, and claim boundary. A draft remains unsent for my review.
Only the owner chooses the inputs. The AI returns evidence before any draft.
AI relationship red flags
  • Vague “just checking in” outreach.
  • Invented familiarity or assumed history.
  • Fake urgency or inferred intent.
  • Unsupported value claims.
  • A newsletter or open treated as relationship proof.
  • Single-thread evidence presented as certainty.

Why save this as a skill

Save the method. Refresh the relationship context every time.

The reusable part is the selection gate, the evidence fields, the ranking rules, the contact flags, and the stop before a draft. That gives every new run the same privacy and approval boundary without requiring the owner to restate it from scratch.

What stays reusablePurpose-first intake, bounded-source rules, the evidence map, uncertainty labels, owner review, and the no-contact stop.
What changes per jobThe relationship goal, time horizon, selected threads or label, current reason to write, and the owner’s final decision.

A saved skill reduces re-explaining. It does not load an inbox, contain a contact list, promise fixed token savings, or create automatic outreach. Each run still starts with owner-authorized context and ends with the owner.

The actual job

A forgotten thread is not a relationship strategy.

A founder’s inbox carries real context: who made an introduction, which project went quiet, where someone asked for a follow-up, and which conversation should stay closed. It also carries newsletters, auto-replies, old accounts, and private material with no business purpose.

That mix creates the bad default: scan harder, remember less, then send a generic “just checking in.” A relationship map gives the owner a smaller decision surface. It makes the evidence, uncertainty, and contact boundary visible before anyone writes.

Gmail has practical ways to narrow a candidate set through labels and search filters.3 An agent that works from Gmail should expose the exact authorization it requests; Google documents scopes as the permissions that define the data and level of access an app asks for.2

Context map. The arrow to a draft stays closed until owner review.

Choose an input that matches the purpose.

Start with a defined relationship job. “Find people to email” is too loose. “Reconnect with former partners I could recognize and give a specific update to this quarter” gives the map something defensible to rank.

Path A

Owner-selected threads

Paste or export text for 10–25 conversations. Give each thread a simple label such as T07 so the map can point back to it.

Path B

One named label

Use a label the owner exports or explicitly authorizes in the agent. A Gmail API list request can be constrained with label IDs when the integration supports it.1

Path C

Relationship list + links

Paste the people/accounts that are already in scope with the relevant thread links or identifiers. The agent reads only what the list points to.

An export can include message content, headers, attachments, and user-defined labels.4 Keep the handoff narrow. If a tool can only export everything, choose Path A or C instead of creating a larger privacy problem.

  • Credentials, tokens, password resets, payment details, health information, legal strategy, and unrelated family or team messages.
  • Private content belonging to someone else, even if it is easy to collect or enrich.
  • Newsletter opens, tracking signals, job titles, or enrichment data presented as proof that a person wants contact.
  • Any thread the owner cannot recognize, explain, or safely review.

Build the map in five controlled moves.

Choose one relationship goal.

Pick a purpose and a time horizon. The goal tells the AI what matters. It does not give it permission to hunt through a mailbox for more names.

Prepare the smallest useful set.

Select one input path. Name the label or link set. Remove content that has no role in the stated decision.

Extract relationship facts with pointers.

For every row, capture relationship context, last substantive contact, and a thread/date pointer. Mark gaps instead of papering them over.

Rank candidates against the purpose.

Rank clear, relevant evidence. Carry forward sensitivity and explicit boundaries. A row can be useful because it says “do not contact.”

Review the evidence before any draft.

The owner keeps, corrects, removes, or blocks each row. A draft starts only when the owner chooses one approved person and supplies current, supportable reasons to write.

Synthetic decision table. The third row is a successful outcome for the map because it prevents a careless follow-up.

Run the taste test before the map becomes work.

The map earns its keep when the owner can make cleaner decisions with less reconstruction. Score the output before it generates a draft.

Evidence qualityCan every material relationship claim point to a selected thread, date, and clear context?
RelevanceDoes each ranked row serve the one purpose the owner chose, or did the list drift into names for names’ sake?
RecencyIs the last substantive contact visible, and is any stale context labeled as stale?
Owner recognitionCan the owner recognize the person and explain why the relationship matters before any outreach is considered?
PrivacyDid the input stay inside the stated purpose, authorized source, and smallest useful set?
Editing burdenDid the map reduce reconstruction, or did the owner have to correct its assumptions row by row?

Stop condition: If the strongest rows rely on vague evidence, the owner does not recognize the context, or sensitivity is unresolved, reduce the input set and rebuild. Do not turn uncertainty into a draft.

Keep the authority line obvious.

AI may do

  • Organize only the selected, authorized context around one purpose.
  • Extract facts with evidence pointers and mark uncertainty.
  • Rank potential next moves and preserve contact boundaries.
  • Prepare one unsent draft after a separate owner choice.

The owner must do

  • Authorize the exact threads, label, or relationship list.
  • Recognize people and verify relationship context.
  • Choose who matters, whether a draft is appropriate, and what it can claim.
  • Edit, send, schedule, add contacts, or do nothing.

Run one small relationship review.

Choose one purpose you can judge this month. Collect 10 threads you would be comfortable reviewing line by line. Copy the prompt, make the map, and block any row that fails the evidence test.
