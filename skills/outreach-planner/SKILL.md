# Outreach Planner

Turn deliberately chosen relationship evidence into a plan the owner can inspect. This skill is a method, not a preloaded contact list, CRM, or autonomous outreach agent.

## 1. Set the boundary before reading anything

Require all four inputs before planning:

1. **One current offer or goal** with the action the owner is trying to earn.
2. **An owner-approved relationship map** that names the people or accounts in scope.
3. **Selected contact or thread facts** for the rows being considered, with a date and evidence pointer or short approved paraphrase.
4. **A clear purpose for each contact.** If the owner cannot state a purpose, keep the row out of the plan or mark it `do not contact`.

Accept only the selected map, linked facts, and current goal. Treat missing, stale, conflicting, private, or sensitive facts as unknown. Ask the owner to remove credentials, payment data, health information, legal strategy, and unrelated personal content before sharing material.

Do not request or inspect an inbox-wide archive, third-party private data, credentials, enrichment data, a contact database, engagement opens, or inferred intent or budget. Do not create contacts, update a CRM, or expand the list.

## 2. Extract evidence, not a story

For each candidate, capture only what the approved material supports:

- person and account;
- relationship evidence: thread or source label, date, and concise factual paraphrase;
- last substantive contact when available;
- current trigger, if it is public and selected by the owner;
- why the owner might write now;
- explicit boundaries, opt-outs, pauses, conflicts, or sensitivity; and
- uncertainty and staleness.

Never turn a job change, funding announcement, event attendance, web visit, open, title, or one vague message into proof of familiarity, interest, budget, urgency, or permission to contact. A public business trigger can explain timing. It does not prove a person wants a pitch.

## 3. Produce the ranked outreach plan

Return the plan before any draft. Use this table and preserve a row even when its useful answer is not to contact:

| Rank | Person / account | Relationship evidence | Why now | Purpose | Suggested move | Confidence | Reason not to contact | Timing suggestion | Do-not-contact | Owner review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use these rules:

- Rank by the stated goal, relationship evidence, a specific present reason, and the plausibility of a small next move. Do not rank by guessed value, seniority, enrichment score, or presumed buying intent.
- Write a suggested move as a reversible owner-reviewed action: verify a public trigger, ask a specific question, send a short relevant update, or leave the relationship alone.
- Use `high`, `medium`, or `low` confidence and explain the limiting fact when it is not high.
- Use `yes` for `Do-not-contact` when the selected evidence shows an opt-out, request to pause, active conflict, owner instruction, unresolved sensitivity, or no supportable purpose. State why.
- Keep timing as a suggestion, such as `after owner confirms the announcement is current` or `not before the event invite is final`. Never place a calendar hold, create a task, or schedule a message.

For a small launch or event list, use the same row structure. Require a distinct purpose and relationship evidence for each person. Do not convert a guest list into a mass-personalized campaign.

## 4. Hold the approval gate

After presenting the plan, ask the owner to mark every row:

- `keep`;
- `correct`;
- `remove`;
- `do not contact`; or
- `approve one draft`.

Only after the owner explicitly chooses `approve one draft` for a named row may you prepare one unsent draft. Then ask for the verified reason for contact, the channel, desired action, current facts or claims, timing preference, and any wording or compliance limit. Attach the supporting facts and open questions to the draft.

Do not draft for a row marked `do not contact`, `remove`, or low-confidence without a further owner correction. Do not schedule or send. If the owner asks to schedule or send, stop and request explicit approval for that exact person, message, channel, and timing; the owner must still take or visibly confirm the consequential action.

## 5. Run the red-flag pass

Flag the exact row or proposed draft and stop or revise when it contains:

- generic “just checking in” language with no specific purpose;
- invented familiarity or a relationship story the evidence does not support;
- mass personalization or copy-pasted follow-ups;
- false urgency or a public trigger presented as private intent;
- inferred budget, buying authority, interest, or timing;
- a claim the owner cannot verify;
- a schedule, send, contact creation, or account change without the required owner approval; or
- pressure tactics, opt-out avoidance, or any reason the person should not be contacted.

State the safer move: verify, ask the owner, narrow the purpose, change the timing, prepare nothing, or mark `do not contact`.

## 6. Score the plan before it becomes work

Score the completed plan from 1–5 for:

- evidence quality;
- purpose clarity;
- current relevance;
- relationship fit;
- boundary handling;
- owner editing burden; and
- usefulness of the no-contact decisions.

Rebuild or reduce the set when material rows lack evidence, rely on guessed intent, need generic copy to work, or blur the approval line. The goal is a cleaner owner decision, not a larger list.
