# Relationship Map

Turn a deliberately selected set of email threads into a decision table the owner can inspect. The output is a method, not a preloaded contact list.

## Typical uses

| Situation | Bounded input | Useful output | Owner check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reconnect from a concrete update | Up to 12 owner-selected threads with former partners from a defined period. | A ranked review list with shared-project or explicit-request evidence. | Can the owner explain why they would write now without inventing familiarity or need? |
| Explore a warm path into one named account | Up to eight people the owner already knows, plus links to their relevant threads. | A map of direct relationship evidence, possible introduction context, and rows needing confirmation. | Does the thread actually support an introduction? |
| Clear a small neglected follow-up queue | One current-task-authorized Gmail label, capped at 25 threads. | A review queue separating explicit commitments from stale context, auto-replies, and contact boundaries. | Is the context current, and does any thread call for pause or no contact? |

## Why save this as a skill

Keep these parts reusable: the purpose-first intake, input limits, evidence fields, ranking rules, uncertainty labels, contact flags, and the stop before a draft. This prevents each new request from turning into a mailbox scan because the workflow boundary is already explicit.

Replace these parts on every job: the relationship goal, time horizon, selected threads or designated label, relevant account, current reason to write, and the owner’s final decision.

Do not claim fixed token savings. A saved skill reduces re-explaining; it does not contain a contact list, access an inbox on its own, or create automatic outreach.

## 1. Get the purpose before the data

Ask the owner to choose one purpose:

- reconnect with people they already know;
- surface possible introductions for one defined goal;
- decide which neglected follow-ups deserve attention; or
- identify a small nurture list after a specific event, project, or milestone.

Do not accept or inspect threads until the owner picks one purpose and names the outcome they want. Keep that purpose visible in the map.

Then ask the owner to choose one input path:

- **A. Selected threads:** Paste or provide exported text for 10–25 owner-selected threads.
- **B. Designated Gmail label:** Read only the named label after the owner explicitly authorizes that narrow access in the current task. State the label, estimated thread count, and requested permission before reading. If the available tool cannot limit itself to that label, stop and use A or C.
- **C. Relationship list:** Paste a simple person/account list with links or identifiers for the relevant threads. Read only the linked threads.

Reject credentials, an inbox dump, a mailbox-wide search, or a list from third-party private content. Ask the owner to remove secrets, payment information, health information, and unrelated sensitive content before sharing text.

## 2. Extract facts with evidence

For every candidate, extract only facts supported by the selected input:

- person and account;
- relationship context and the owner’s role;
- last **substantive** contact, including date when available;
- direct evidence with a thread label, date, and short paraphrase or approved excerpt;
- explicit commitments, introductions, boundaries, or opt-outs; and
- uncertainty, sensitivity, staleness, or conflicting evidence.

Treat a newsletter, open, auto-reply, calendar invite, or a single vague thread as weak evidence. Do not infer familiarity, buying intent, availability, a referral promise, or urgency.

## 3. Build the relationship map

Return a ranked table with these columns:

| Rank | Person / account | Relationship context | Last substantive contact | Evidence | Suggested next move | Confidence | Sensitivity | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Use one of these flags in every row:

- **ready for owner review** — evidence is clear enough for the owner to decide;
- **needs owner confirmation** — a material fact, current relevance, or relationship context is uncertain;
- **do not contact** — the selected material shows an opt-out, a boundary, an active conflict, or a reason not to contact;
- **insufficient evidence** — keep out of the ranked candidates until more owner-authorized evidence exists.

Rank only by the chosen purpose, evidence quality, recency, explicit relationship context, and a plausible owner-controlled next move. Do not use engagement opens, enrichment scores, inferred seniority, or a model guess as relationship proof. Keep high-sensitivity rows visible but unranked unless the owner asks to review them.

## 4. Stop at owner review

Show the evidence map first. Ask the owner to mark each row: keep, correct, remove, do not contact, or consider a draft. Explain that “consider a draft” permits a draft only after the owner selects the person, channel, timing, and claim.

Do not create contacts, update a CRM, enrich records, send a message, schedule a meeting, add a task, post, submit, purchase, or change an account. Do not draft outreach for a row marked `needs owner confirmation`, `do not contact`, or `insufficient evidence`.

## 5. Draft only after a separate owner choice

If the owner chooses one approved row, ask for:

- the verified reason for contacting them now;
- the desired channel and action;
- any current fact or value claim the owner can support; and
- the owner’s preferred timing and tone.

Produce one unsent draft with its source facts and open questions. Flag vague “just checking in,” invented familiarity, fake urgency, inferred intent, unsupported value claims, newsletter/open evidence, and certainty built on one thread. The owner decides whether to edit, send, schedule, or do nothing.
