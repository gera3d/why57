# Social Content Strategy

Turn a bounded set of owned published posts into evidence the owner can inspect. This skill provides a method, not a preloaded strategy, audience, posting calendar, or automated publishing system.

## 1. Use the method for a concrete content decision

| Situation | Bounded input | Useful output | Owner check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LinkedIn teaching posts repeat the problem but rarely name the offer. | 12 owner-selected LinkedIn posts from one quarter plus the current offer in one sentence. | Map repeated operational-friction stories, audience language, and missing offer or CTA coverage. | Confirm the intended audience and current offer before approving one CTA experiment. |
| X posts or selected Reddit threads show a recurring objection the offer can actually help solve. | 10–20 owner-selected post or thread URLs plus the current offer in one sentence. | Separate direct problem language from a guess about demand, and identify one helpful contribution or content direction. | Confirm community rules, the intended audience, and whether a public contribution is appropriate before drafting anything. |
| A Facebook launch sequence may be a repeatable pattern or a one-off. | 10–15 selected Facebook posts from one launch and only owner-verified metrics. | Separate observed topic/format repetition from a hypothesis about the launch job. | Decide which posts still support the offer and whether one dated test is worth running. |
| One high-reaction post is pulling the next calendar off course. | 10 selected posts, including that post, and owner-verified metrics only if available. | Preserve reactions as an observed signal and leave any business outcome unproven unless the owner verifies it. | Choose whether to test, update, or leave the idea alone. |

## 2. Save the method, not a strategy

Keep these reusable across jobs:

- the three authorized input paths;
- the Post Evidence Table and its observed-versus-inference distinction;
- the Content Pattern Map fields, red-flag pass, and approval gate; and
- the rule that the owner verifies context, chooses the strategy, and publishes or schedules personally.

Ask for these afresh for each job: platform, business goal, offer, audience, selected post set, verified metrics, constraints, and any proposed experiment date.

Saving this as a skill reduces re-explaining the method and protects the workflow boundary when the next review begins. It does not promise fixed token savings, a preloaded strategy, or automatic publishing.

## 3. Set the review boundary

Ask these questions before reading any content:

> Choose one platform: LinkedIn, X, Reddit, or Facebook. Choose one current business goal: [offer awareness / qualified conversations / customer education / recruiting / another specific goal]. Then choose one authorized input: A. an export of your own published posts, B. 10–30 public post or thread URLs you are authorized to review, or C. pasted post text with date, format, and any metrics you choose to share.

Accept only:

- **A. Authorized export:** the owner has selected and supplied it. Read only published-post material needed for the review; keep private messages, contacts, credentials, and unrelated account data out.
- **B. 10–30 public post or thread URLs:** read only the supplied URLs. Do not expand to other creators, followers, commenters, profiles, or recommendations. For Reddit, flag supplied community rules as a condition before suggesting any contribution; never treat a community as a distribution list.
- **C. Pasted post list:** require post text, date, format, and any owner-approved metrics. Treat missing metrics as unknown.

Never ask for credentials, a full account connection, private DMs, a login session, other creators' content, or permission to publish. Stop if the owner cannot authorize the material or name the business goal.

## 4. Normalize post evidence

Create one evidence row per post before interpreting patterns. Capture:

| Field | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Post / date / platform | Use the supplied URL or owner label and the original date. |
| Topic | Name the concrete subject, not a vague theme. |
| Audience | Use stated audience language; otherwise mark it inferred. |
| Format | Label text, image, carousel/document, video, link, poll, or another supplied format. |
| Hook | Record the opening move without copying large blocks. |
| Point of view | State the claim, lens, or teaching move; mark uncertain when unclear. |
| CTA | Record an explicit CTA, or write `none visible`. |
| Metrics | Keep only owner-verified values and their period. Do not fill gaps. |

Separate direct observations from inferences. Cite the post label or URL for each claim. Do not call a metric a business result unless the owner supplied a verified business outcome and its attribution is clear.

## 5. Build the Content Pattern Map

Return a map with these sections, in order:

1. **Observable patterns** — repetitions in topic, audience, format, hook, point of view, CTA, and cadence; include supporting post labels and confidence (high / medium / low).
2. **Strategy hypotheses** — explain what the pattern may be trying to achieve and why it remains a hypothesis. Do not infer intent from one post.
3. **Gaps** — identify missing or thin topic, audience, format, CTA, or offer coverage against the owner’s current goal. Label each gap observed, inferred, or unknown.
4. **Decisions** — give every important pattern one decision and its evidence: `keep`, `change`, `test`, `retire`, `update`, or `leave alone`.
5. **One dated experiment** — propose one small test with a date, named audience, topic, format, CTA, business-goal link, owner-provided baseline if any, and a review date. State that the experiment is a proposal, not a posting instruction.

Use a table when it improves review. Include these columns for each meaningful pattern: `pattern | evidence | observed signal | inference | likely job | confidence | keep/change/test/retire | owner question`.

## 6. Hold the approval gate

Stop after the map and ask the owner to mark each pattern: **keep, change, test, retire, update, leave alone, or insufficient evidence**.

Do not produce a strategy, content calendar, or draft until the owner explicitly approves that next stage. Then ask for:

- current business goal;
- offer and desired action;
- audience and decision context;
- channel, capacity, voice, legal, privacy, or timing constraints; and
- verified performance or business data the owner wants considered.

Treat a draft as an unpublished working document. The owner approves every post and personally publishes or schedules it.

## 7. Run the red-flag pass

Flag and correct these failures before showing the map or any later draft:

- treating likes, reactions, or reach as revenue or proof of demand;
- inferring a durable strategy from one viral post;
- copying another creator's voice, structure, or content;
- offering generic “post more” advice;
- inventing audience intent, business outcomes, or missing metrics;
- optimizing for reach when the business goal is unspecified; and
- converting a research insight into an automatic posting plan.
- treating a Reddit community or an X reply thread as a place to extract leads rather than a place to add a genuinely useful contribution;

State the post or pattern behind each flag, the reason it fails, and the safer revision or stop condition.

## 8. Preserve authority and score the result

| AI may do | Owner must do |
| --- | --- |
| Classify authorized posts, surface patterns, name uncertainty, and propose experiments. | Authorize data, verify metrics and context, choose the strategy, and approve every post. |
| Draft only after explicit approval and a current brief. | Publish or schedule personally. |

Before advancing, score the map 1–5 for strategic relevance, evidence quality, business-goal fit, audience clarity, originality, privacy, and editing burden. Rebuild the map when it cannot point to evidence, drifts from the goal, relies on unverified metrics, or requires the owner to rewrite the central logic.
