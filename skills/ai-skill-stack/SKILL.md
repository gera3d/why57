# AI Skill Stack

Build a small operating stack around work the owner already repeats. A skill is the reusable method. The current job still needs current facts, a defined output, and an owner review.

## 1. Set the boundary before mapping

Ask for only these inputs:

- the 3–6 recurring jobs the owner actually performs;
- the result each job should produce;
- the two or three workflows or skills that have worked well enough to reuse;
- the minimal current inputs each job needs; and
- the owner, review point, and consequences to avoid.

Use pasted, uploaded, or explicitly authorized material only. Do not request a full archive, credentials, private inbox, account connection, or background access. Do not infer that a connected tool is needed.

State these exclusions at the start: no autonomous outreach, automatic scheduling, payment, publishing, submissions, access changes, or external representation. A recommendation is not an action.

## 2. Choose the stack by job, not by product

Select two or three skills that each have one clear job. A candidate is proven when the owner has used its method on at least one bounded task and can describe what made the output usable or unusable.

Do not use one mega-prompt as the default for every job. Do not add a skill because a tool can connect to it. Do not scale a workflow until one bounded task has passed review.

Use this routing test for each candidate:

| Test | Keep the skill when | Stop or narrow when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recurrence | The job appears often enough to justify reusable instructions. | It is a one-off or its goal keeps changing. |
| Distinct method | Its steps, inputs, and quality bar differ from the other skills. | It repeats another skill under a new name. |
| Current context | The job can run on a small, named set of current inputs. | It depends on a full archive, guesses, or stale material. |
| Review | A named owner can judge the output before it represents anyone. | No one can verify the facts, commitments, or audience fit. |
| Learning | Feedback can become one specific rule or stop condition. | The workflow produces noise with no decision or correction. |

## 3. Produce the Skill Stack Map

Create one table. Keep the wording operational and specific.

| Recurring job | Chosen skill | Minimum current inputs | Expected output | Review gate | Stop condition | Feedback loop |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [job] | [one skill] | [smallest current facts or approved artifacts] | [reviewable recommendation, brief, or draft] | [who checks what] | [what missing fact, risk, or ambiguity stops work] | [where the owner’s correction becomes a rule] |

For each row, identify whether the AI is asked to:

- summarize or classify evidence;
- propose a decision, plan, or draft; or
- prepare a review packet.

Do not label a row “automate.” Name the reviewable output instead. If the proposed output could send, publish, schedule, pay, submit, change access, or represent the owner, stop at a recommendation or draft and name the owner action separately.

## 4. Route the work with current context

For an approved row, load the method plus only the row’s current inputs. State the evidence, date, source, and uncertainty for every material point. Keep reusable rules separate from this week’s facts.

Return this review packet:

1. **Route used** — recurring job and selected skill.
2. **Current inputs used** — source label, date, and why each input belongs.
3. **Output** — the requested brief, recommendation, or draft.
4. **Evidence and uncertainty** — verified facts, assumptions, missing information, and conflicts.
5. **Owner review** — the exact decisions or checks required.
6. **Stop status** — proceed to owner review, narrow the task, or stop.

Ask for approval before drafting any external-facing material or doing consequential work. Never send, publish, schedule, purchase, submit, change access, or contact anyone.

## 5. Start with one small operating loop

Use a bounded first test. These are synthetic patterns, not claimed results:

| Operating loop | Possible skill | Minimal current inputs | Reviewable output | Owner check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Founder weekly rhythm | Weekly Operating Brief | active priorities, one-week calendar summary supplied by the owner, current constraints, last decisions | decision list, risks, and proposed next actions | priorities, commitments, and what to defer |
| Small sales and marketing | Signal-to-Draft | approved account signals, offer, verified proof, and audience | ranked outreach or content angles; optional unsent draft after approval | relevance, factual claims, and who should receive it |
| Client delivery coordination | Delivery Handoff | approved scope, current work status, blockers, and client commitments | status brief, open decisions, and handoff checklist | scope, promises, and client-facing language |

Select no more than three rows for the first map. Run one row once. Compare the output against the owner’s usual process, then keep, change, or remove one rule.

## 6. Run the taste test and update one rule

Ask the owner to score the result:

- **Right route:** Did the selected skill fit the job?
- **Right context:** Was every input relevant and current?
- **Evidence:** Are claims traceable and uncertainty visible?
- **Review burden:** Did this create a useful check, or more cleanup?
- **Boundary:** Did it stop before a consequential action?

When the owner gives feedback, record one of: keep, change, remove, or add a stop condition. Update the reusable method only after the owner approves the change. Do not turn one successful output into permission to broaden access or autonomy.

## 7. Flag red flags plainly

Stop and explain the issue if the request involves:

- one mega-prompt attempting to run every job;
- irrelevant context piled in “just in case”;
- an agent recommendation treated as an approved action;
- a tool connection treated as the strategy;
- a workflow being scaled before one task works;
- a full archive, credentials, or unapproved private data; or
- an attempt to send, schedule, publish, pay, submit, change access, or do outreach automatically.

Deliver a completed Skill Stack Map and the next owner decision. Do not claim the stack is autonomous or preloaded with the owner’s data.
