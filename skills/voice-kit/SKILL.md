# Voice Kit

Turn selected writing into rules the owner can inspect and change. A Voice Kit is not an exact voice clone and it is not a reason to load an entire archive.

## 1. Choose the smallest useful starting source

Ask exactly:

> Pick one starting source:
> A. 1-3 public YouTube links where I explain or teach.
> B. 3-5 posts or articles I wrote.
> C. One longer email I wrote.
> Pick one.

- For public YouTube links, obtain the public transcript. If it is unavailable, ask the owner to paste it.
- For posts or articles, read only the links supplied.
- For an email, require names and sensitive details to be removed first.
- Ask for a second sample only to test a pattern that is uncertain.

Treat speech and writing as different evidence. Speech shows how the person reasons. Posts and emails show how they write for a specific channel.

Label every sample with its audience, job, channel, date, and purpose.

## 2. Build the Author Writing Sheet

Create the sheet before drafting. Every rule must include:

- observed pattern;
- source label and a short supporting excerpt;
- applicable channel;
- confidence: repeated, one-off, or uncertain; and
- instruction for the draft.

Cover the opening move, argument order, sentence length, vocabulary, examples, directness, certainty, questions, endings, and what sounds unlike the owner. Do not use vague labels such as "professional," "human," or "authentic." When sources conflict, mark the rule channel-specific or uncertain.

Show the Author Writing Sheet and wait for the owner to mark each rule: keep, change, or remove. Do not draft before approval.

## 3. Build the Voice Kit after approval

Include:

- the approved Author Writing Sheet;
- channel-specific rules;
- a rule to select no more than three relevant samples for a new task;
- a drafting prompt that names the rules it used; and
- a review form: more like me, less like me, keep, remove, or never use again.

Do not blend conflicting channels into one rule. Keep approved reusable rules separate from current task context.

## 4. Draft from a current brief

Ask for:

- reader or audience;
- desired action;
- verified facts;
- limits or claims to avoid; and
- channel.

Do not infer missing facts. Load only the current brief and the samples that match the job.

For the first task, produce a baseline draft and a Voice Kit draft. Name the rules that changed the second draft. Score both from 1-5 for recognizability, reasoning, channel fit, accuracy, editing burden, and privacy. Flag what the owner must verify. Revise the kit from owner feedback.

## 5. Run the AI-writing red-flag pass

Before showing a draft, flag and rewrite these patterns unless the approved samples show the owner uses them:

- generic openers or closers, including "I hope you're well," "I wanted to reach out," and "Looking forward to hearing from you";
- fake contrast, including "This isn't X. It's Y.";
- fake reader dialogue or rhetorical bait, including "You may be thinking" and "The question is";
- empty intensifiers, including "game-changing," "powerful," "seamless," and "transformative";
- corporate filler, including "leverage," "unlock," "streamline," and "optimize" without a concrete action;
- formulaic three-item lists, "Key takeaways," or headings the source author does not use; and
- em dashes, polished signoffs, or certainty the Author Writing Sheet does not support.

For each red flag, identify the sentence, the flag, and the rewrite. Use the approved sheet for the rewrite. Do not swap one template phrase for another.

## 6. Hold the boundary

- Do not ask for or inspect an inbox, drive, full archive, passwords, or private messages.
- Do not use other people's writing as the owner's samples.
- Do not copy sample sentences, invent facts, or claim an exact voice clone.
- Do not use every sample for every task or use stale samples that no longer represent the owner.
- Do not send, publish, submit, spend money, or change an account.

Deliver the approved Voice Kit as a reusable skill or a clean Markdown document, depending on the platform. The owner reviews every external-facing draft and takes every consequential action.
