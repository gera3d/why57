AI Executive Cookbook · Recipe 8

Build a proof library from work you already completed.

Turn approved project notes, launch records, customer feedback, and outcomes into source-linked proof cards. Each card says what you can say, where it came from, what it does not prove, and who still has to approve it.

See the use cases

Use only material you own or are authorized to use. A clean card is not approval to publish it.

Recipe at a glance

Keep the evidence close to the claim.

Daily job
Find a credible example without rereading every old project folder.
Inputs
Only selected, approved project notes, launch records, feedback, outcomes, and permission notes.
Output
Proof cards with claim, source, scope, date, permission, allowed wording, and limits.
Handoff
The owner verifies the source, approves disclosure and wording, then decides whether to use it.

Where this is useful

One source set. Three honest ways to use it.

The library starts with a bounded job. That keeps a project fact from becoming a broad promise.

Sales responseAnswer a buyer asking, “Have you done something like this?”

Bounded input: the owner-selected project summary, delivery record, and approval note for one comparable engagement.

Useful output: a short source-linked answer with safe wording and open questions.

Owner check: confirm the comparison is fair and the client reference is permitted.

Capability pageFind examples that support one service without overclaiming.

Bounded input: 3–6 approved records tied to a specific capability.

Useful output: a small set of anonymized or approved-named examples, each with a clear scope.

Owner check: approve every name, logo, quote, metric, and public wording.

Case study outlineStart the first draft from records that can carry the weight.

Bounded input: approved discovery notes, delivery milestones, launch evidence, outcome record, and disclosure status.

Useful output: a case-study outline that leaves unknowns visible instead of filling them with sales copy.

Owner check: verify sequence, causal language, customer quote, and approval for publication.

Why save this as a skill

Save the method. Load the proof for the current job.

What repeats

The proof-card fields, source check, wording guardrails, stale-proof check, and owner approval gate. OpenAI describes a skill as a reusable package of instructions and optional resources; that is the reusable part here.1

What changes

The selected records, buyer question, page section, case-study angle, dates, permissions, and what the owner wants to disclose. The skill holds the process without pretending it holds your client history.

Ready-made portable skillhttps://why57.com/skills/proof-library/SKILL.md

It is a method, not preloaded data or an autonomous agent. Open the file, save it as skills/proof-library/SKILL.md (or the equivalent skills folder), and tell your AI to read it before working from approved records.

Copyable promptOwner approval required
# Goal: build a source-linked proof library from work I already completed

Build a reviewable proof library from only the project notes, launch records, customer feedback, outcome records, and approval notes I explicitly provide or authorize for this task. Do not ask for an inbox dump, full drive, credentials, raw customer data, confidential material, unapproved names/logos, or any record I cannot substantiate.

Start by asking me for:
1. one intended use: a credible sales response, safe capability-page examples, or a first case-study outline;
2. a selected set of authorized source records, each with a link or file label, date if known, project scope, and any known permission or restriction; and
3. the disclosure level I want to consider: internal only, anonymized, or named client if separately approved.

For each supportable fact, create one proof card with:
- Proof ID;
- claim;
- source link, file label, or record ID plus a locating note;
- source date;
- project scope, role, deliverable, period, and conditions;
- evidence type;
- permission status;
- allowed wording;
- what cannot be claimed;
- freshness: current, dated but usable with date, or stale—recheck; and
- owner review note.

Keep a direct source pointer with every card. State uncertainty plainly. Separate what was delivered, an observed outcome, customer feedback, and inference. Do not inflate metrics, take credit for work the client did, infer causation from timing alone, turn one project fact into a broad promise, use stale proof without flagging it, disclose a named client without approval, or fabricate a testimonial.

Exclude and label as do not use: confidential or restricted work, raw credentials, unapproved names/logos/screenshots, unsupported results, and any claim the owner cannot substantiate.

Return first:
1. the proof-card table;
2. a review queue with status: ready for owner review, needs owner confirmation, or do not use; and
3. a short list of missing evidence, permissions, or causal/metric concerns.

Ask me to approve, correct, anonymize, or remove selected proof cards before drafting a sales response, capability-page example, or case-study outline. After approval, create only the requested draft and retain source pointers for internal review. Do not send, publish, post, submit, change an account, or represent a customer.
No customer content is sent to analytics.

The recipeGood proof is narrower than a sales claim. The limitation belongs on the card, not buried after the copy is written.

The recipe

Build the library in five passes.

Choose one use.

State whether the immediate job is a buyer response, capability page, or case-study outline. It determines the relevant proof and approval needed.

Select only authorized records.

Label the source, its date, scope, and known disclosure restriction. Keep restricted material out before any extraction begins.

Make one narrow proof card per fact.

Record the claim beside its source link, scope, date, permission, allowed wording, and what the fact cannot prove.

Test permission, freshness, and causation.

Flag missing client approval, a stale outcome, a correlation dressed up as cause, and any metric without its record.

Stop for owner review.

Use only cards the owner approves for the exact channel. Then draft one bounded asset without sending, publishing, or representing the customer.

Use when all four are clear

  • The source can be opened and located.
  • The wording preserves scope and date.
  • Permission covers this channel and identifier level.
  • The card does not imply a missing outcome or causal result.

Stop and recheck when one is missing

  • A metric appears in a slide but has no original record.
  • The client did the work that produced the outcome.
  • The activity and result happened together, but causation is unknown.
  • The client name, quote, logo, or record is not approved for this use.

Owner approval

AI can organize evidence. It cannot grant permission.

The FTC’s current guidance is a practical reminder: a business that features a testimonial on its own site is disseminating a promotional message, and false or fabricated testimonials create real risk.3 Use that standard well before the page goes live.

AI may do

  • Extract facts from the owner-selected source set.
  • Build source-linked cards and flag uncertainty.
  • Suggest narrower wording, anonymization, and freshness labels.
  • Prepare a reviewable sales response, capability example, or outline after card approval.

Owner must do

  • Verify source accuracy, scope, and any metric.
  • Approve the wording, name/logo/quote use, and channel.
  • Decide whether a causal inference is fair.
  • Choose whether to send, publish, submit, share, or do nothing.

Taste test

Can a skeptical reviewer trace and defend the card?

  • I can open the source and find this exact fact.
  • The project, role, date, condition, and permission status are visible.
  • The allowed wording does not promise a broader result or typical outcome.
  • The draft does not take credit for the client’s own work or assert causation without evidence.
  • The named or anonymized use matches the owner’s approval.

Red flags

Remove or hold the card when you see:

  • Metric inflation or a number without a source.
  • “We drove” language where the record only shows timing or delivery.
  • Old proof used as if it describes the current offer.
  • A client name, logo, quote, or screenshot without approval.
  • A quotation invented from a customer’s likely sentiment.
