# Voice Kit: make AI draft like me, not like AI

Give me a draft in my actual voice from whatever real samples I share, a YouTube link, a doc, a pasted email, even one paragraph. Don't run an interview before drafting.

The one idea that matters: a voice kit built only from abstracted rules ("be direct," "avoid jargon") produces AI-sounding output, because a model given a rule like that invents new phrasing that satisfies the rule instead of recalling my actual words. The fix: keep my real sample text on hand, and check anything punchy-sounding against it before using it.

How to work:
1. Take whatever I give you, immediately. One sample is enough to start. If more would sharpen it, mention that once after the first draft, not before.
2. Draft from my current request right away if it already states the audience, channel, and topic. Only ask a direct question when something you actually need for the draft is missing.

Two ways I can give you a sample, either is fine:
- I paste or link it: a YouTube URL, a blog post, a pasted email, a transcript.
- I say "just go look": if you have a connected browser already signed into my own accounts, navigate to a page I name, my YouTube channel, my blog, my LinkedIn, and read it directly. Only visit pages I name as my own. Don't wander to unrelated pages or follow outbound links looking for more, and don't sign into anything new. Stay off my inbox, DMs, drafts, and account settings even if the session could reach them, "look at my blog" means the blog.
3. Pull a short list of real, observable patterns from my sample (an actual verbal tic, a real intensifier, a real structural move), and show it next to the draft, not as a form I have to approve before you're allowed to write.
4. State your confidence in one line if it's based on a single sample. Don't block on it, and don't guess at patterns you don't have evidence for.

Before showing me a draft, check it against my real sample:
- If a sentence sounds notably punchy, clever, or quotable and doesn't resemble real phrasing in my sample, it's probably invented, not my voice. Cut it and say the plain version instead, even if that reads as less polished.
- Also cut, unless my sample shows I actually use them: generic openers/closers ("I hope you're well," "Looking forward to hearing from you"), fake contrast ("This isn't X, it's Y"), empty intensifiers ("game-changing," "seamless," "transformative"), corporate filler ("leverage," "unlock," "streamline"), em dashes, and formulaic three-item lists the source doesn't use.

For LinkedIn, X, or other short-form/social posts specifically:
- Max two short sentences per block or slide, one idea per block.
- If a product or my own work is part of the story, the shape is: a real detail, then the evidence, then what was learned, then what now works, then a confident, low-pressure ask. A mistake can be one detail inside that arc, never the framing of the whole piece.
- If there's an accompanying carousel or visual, match its beats to the caption 1:1.

What you don't do: read my full inbox, drive, or archive, use anyone else's writing as my sample, or send, publish, submit, spend money, or change an account. Automatically redact any other person's name or sensitive detail you find in a sample before using it in a pattern list or draft, don't ask me to pre-redact first. I review and take every consequential action myself.

If I give direct feedback ("I don't write like that"), that always overrides an inferred pattern. Log it with the date and my own words.

What it is useful for

Use it where the words actually matter.

Voice Kit is for the work that needs to sound like you, not like a competent default. It gives your AI a real reference, a current job, and a way to show its work before the draft represents you.

Outbound email

Start with a message you would actually send.

Bring one real email or transcript and the person you are writing to. Get a draft with the source-based choices visible, so you can fix the logic or tone before it goes anywhere.

Post or carousel

Keep the writing and visual beats together.

Bring a post, talk, or rough story. Get short blocks that follow the source's actual rhythm, with a caption and visual beats that match in the same order.

Client explanation

Make a clear point without sounding borrowed.

Bring a strong explanation and the facts for this job. Get a draft that keeps the owner's style separate from the claims that still need verification.

The failure this is designed to avoid.

Abstract instructions such as “be direct” or “avoid jargon” can still produce a clean draft you would never say. In testing, short quoted fragments and abstracted rules led to a made-up punctuation habit and an idiom the writer had never used. Keeping a real sample available made both misses easy to spot and remove.

What the skill actually does.

Voice Kit does not manufacture a personality from a label. It reads a real source you chose, uses that evidence to make a first draft for the job in front of it, then gives you visible pattern notes to correct. Your correction becomes the useful part of the method for the next draft.

Why this approach is worth trying.

The research below supports the pieces of this method. It does not prove this exact skill will create a specific business outcome, save a fixed amount of time, or make every draft sound right on the first pass.

Relevant context

Personal history can improve task-specific generation.

LaMP tested retrieval from a person's history across seven personalized tasks, including email-subject generation and tweet paraphrasing. That supports selecting a real source that fits the current job instead of relying on a generic “sound like me” request.4

It does not prove: reply rates, sales results, or that your first email is ready to send.

Real source text

Style is hard to imitate from a thin summary.

A large evaluation found models handled structured email better than nuanced blogs and forums when trying to imitate everyday authors. That supports keeping the real sample available and treating a single draft as something to inspect, not as an authority.8

It does not prove: that one sample captures every part of a person's voice.

Owner feedback

Explicit preferences and refinement have a role.

Research on personalized writing agents supports checking inferred preferences across samples and refining them over time. That is why direct feedback overrides an inferred pattern in this skill.10

It does not prove: that the AI should act without your review.

Why make this a skill instead of saving one prompt?

A prompt can solve one assignment. A skill keeps the repeatable method: read real source text, draft first, show the pattern notes, run the red-flag pass, and improve from your feedback. The current job and source are what change each time.11

Keep the context bounded.

Select the material that fits the task instead of dumping an archive into every prompt. Research on context compression supports the general efficiency case, but it did not test this Voice Kit or promise a fixed cost saving on every platform.12

What to bring.

One real sample starts the work. More material is optional—use it when the first draft tells you the current sample is too formal, too old, or simply not representative of the job.19

How it reads the sample.

Writing style has observable patterns, including word choice and sentence structure.2 The skill names only the patterns it can point to in the source: a repeated phrase, a sentence-length habit, a real opener, a qualification move, or an actual way of making a point.

Show the pattern notes with the draft.

A 2025 story-generation study supports making implicit style traits explicit and checking them with human feedback. Its story data does not make a claim about business email. The useful part here is visibility: you can correct a bad pattern after seeing a usable first draft, rather than approving a long theory of your voice before the work starts.7

One sample starts the kit. It does not prove every rule. In a 40,000-generation evaluation spanning more than 400 authors, models handled structured email better than nuanced blogs and forums.8

Check each rule across samples and revise it from feedback. A 2025 email-writing study found iterative refinement plus cross-sample verification improved its writing-agent quality over its comparison method.10

  • Skip third-party messages. They are not your voice samples.
  • Sensitive details get redacted automatically. Other people's names and private details are stripped before use, you don't have to pre-clean samples yourself.
  • Skip stale samples. They may reflect an old offer or tone.
  • Skip copied writing. You do not own it.

The skill, step by step.

Ask for the actual draft.

Name the writing job. If the request already has the reader, channel, topic, and facts, the skill should start there instead of making you complete an intake form.

Share one real sample, or let it look.

Paste a link or doc, or if your agent has a connected browser, say “check my YouTube channel” or “look at my blog.” One sample is enough to start.

Get the first draft and its pattern notes.

The skill drafts from what you gave it, then shows the real patterns it used beside the draft. It does not make you approve a writing sheet before it writes anything.

Correct what is off.

Fix a wrong pattern in plain language. Add a stronger or additional sample only if you want a more complete kit for the next job.

Run a side-by-side test when the work matters.

For a repeat assignment, compare a normal AI draft with the Voice Kit draft. Keep the method only when it reduces meaningful editing without creating accuracy or privacy failures.

Drafting rule

Use the Voice Kit. Preserve facts. Flag gaps. Run the red-flag pass. Name what I must verify. Do not send.
