# Build my evidence-backed Voice Kit

Build a reusable skill that drafts [emails / posts / explanations] in my voice. Do not draft until I approve the writing sheet.

Start by asking exactly this:
"Pick one starting source:
A. 1-3 public YouTube links where I explain or teach.
B. 3-5 posts or articles I wrote.
C. One longer email I wrote.
Pick one."

For YouTube, get the public transcript. If you cannot, ask me to paste it. For posts, read only the links I give you. For an email, tell me to remove names and sensitive details. Ask for a second sample only when you need to check an uncertain pattern.

Treat speech and writing differently. Speech shows how I reason. Posts and emails show how I write for a channel. Label every sample with its audience, job, channel, date, and purpose.

Create an Author Writing Sheet. Every rule must have:
- the observed pattern;
- the source label and a short supporting excerpt;
- the channel where it applies;
- confidence: repeated, one-off, or uncertain; and
- the instruction for a draft.

Cover: opening move, argument order, sentence length, vocabulary, examples, directness, certainty, questions, endings, and what sounds unlike me. Do not use vague labels such as "professional" or "human." If samples conflict, mark the rule as channel-specific or uncertain.

Show me the sheet first. I will mark each rule: keep, change, or remove. Then build "My Voice Kit" with:
- the approved Author Writing Sheet;
- channel-specific rules;
- a rule for selecting up to 3 relevant samples for each new task;
- a drafting prompt that cites the rules it used; and
- a review form: more like me, less like me, keep, remove, or never use again.

After I approve the sheet, ask for one current brief:
- reader or audience;
- desired action;
- verified facts;
- limits or claims to avoid; and
- channel.

Do not infer missing facts.

Test the kit on one current brief. Make a baseline draft and a Voice-Kit draft. State the rules that changed the second draft. Score both from 1-5 for recognizability, reasoning, channel fit, accuracy, editing burden, and privacy. Flag missing facts and what I must verify. Revise the kit from my feedback.

## AI WRITING RED-FLAG PASS
Before you show a draft, flag and rewrite these patterns unless my approved samples show I use them:
- generic openers or closers: "I hope you're well," "I wanted to reach out," or "Looking forward to hearing from you";
- fake contrast: "This isn't X. It's Y.";
- fake reader dialogue or rhetorical bait: "You may be thinking..." or "The question is...";
- empty intensifiers: "game-changing," "powerful," "seamless," or "transformative";
- corporate filler: "leverage," "unlock," "streamline," or "optimize" without a concrete action;
- formulaic three-item lists, "Key takeaways," or headings the source author does not use;
- an em dash, polished signoff, or level of certainty that the Author Writing Sheet does not support.

For each red flag, name the sentence, the flag, and the rewrite. Use the approved writing sheet for the rewrite. Do not replace one template phrase with another.

## HARD BOUNDARIES
- Do not ask for my inbox, drive, full archive, passwords, or private messages.
- Do not use other people's writing as my samples.
- Do not copy sample sentences, invent facts, or claim an exact voice clone.
- Do not blend conflicting channels into one rule.
- Do not use every sample for every task.
- Do not use stale samples that no longer represent my offer or writing.
- Do not send, publish, submit, spend money, or change an account.

Save the skill for my review if this platform supports skills. Otherwise give me a clean document I can save.

The problem

A clean draft can still be unusable.

An AI can write a clean email you would never send. The grammar works. The reasoning, certainty, or tone does not.

"Write in my voice" gives the model a label. A few good examples show it how you think.

Use a small, labelled set from the job you need to do. Keep the judgment.

Build a sheet before a draft.

A 2025 story-generation study used a human-validated Author Writing Sheet. Across 3,600 stories from 112 authors, it beat a non-personalized baseline 78% of the time on past-style capture. Story data is not business-email data. The useful method is explicit traits, rules, and human validation.7

One sample starts the kit. It does not prove every rule. In a 40,000-generation evaluation spanning more than 400 authors, models handled structured email better than nuanced blogs and forums.8

Check each rule across samples and revise it from feedback. A 2025 email-writing study found iterative refinement plus cross-sample verification improved its writing-agent quality over its comparison method.10

  • Skip third-party messages. They are not your voice samples.
  • Redact sensitive details. Keep only what the job needs.
  • Skip stale samples. They may reflect an old offer or tone.
  • Skip copied writing. You do not own it.
