First step

Create the evidence and review packet before the working draft.

This prompt builds a daily operating card around one approved method. It pauses before drafting external-facing language or doing consequential work.

# Goal: run one approved AI recipe as a reviewable daily workflow

Use this workflow only for one current, bounded job. Do not turn it into an autonomous workday.

First, ask me to provide or confirm this operating card:
- Trigger: what specific event starts this run?
- Approved skill or method: what single tested recipe may you use?
- Current authorized inputs: exact sources, dates, and why each belongs here.
- Expected output: decision brief, relationship review, or client-update draft package.
- Owner review gate: what facts and judgments must I check?
- Stop condition: what missing fact, stale input, authority boundary, or request ends this run?

Accept only current, authorized inputs. Keep verified facts, inferences, and unknowns separate. Do not load an inbox, private archive, credentials, every available tool, unrelated files, or stale summaries.

Before drafting client-facing language or doing consequential work, give me an Evidence and Review Packet with:
1. The completed operating card.
2. Inputs used, each labeled with source and date.
3. Verified facts, inferences, and unknowns.
4. The reviewable output or a draft outline tied to those facts.
5. The owner checks and the stop condition.

Then ask exactly: "Approve this evidence and scope before I draft or take any consequential action?"

Wait for my approval. After approval, create only the reviewable draft or brief I approved. Do not send, publish, schedule, purchase, submit, change an account, alter access, or represent me externally.

End with a feedback note: what I kept or changed; why; a proposed method update, if any; and status: pending owner approval, approved, or declined.
Do not apply a proposed method update unless I explicitly approve it. A one-off correction remains feedback about this run.

Hard stops: unverified material fact; unauthorized or private input; unclear decision owner; request for unattended messaging, purchases, scheduling, account changes, or external action; or output that creates more review work than it saves.

Start by asking for the Trigger and Approved skill or method.

Daily workflow red flags
  • Loading every tool or archive before the job is defined.
  • Promoting one prompt adjustment into a permanent rule.
  • Hiding approval inside a background step.
  • Counting drafts, runs, or tasks as useful output.

The problem

A prompt that worked once can still fail at 9:15 on a Tuesday.

The meeting is over. You need a decision brief. The facts are in one selected record, a few current project details, and your own judgment about what matters.

That is a real use for AI. The workflow breaks when the method has no trigger, the input boundary expands, or the output moves forward without a review gate.

Give the recipe an operating card. Then judge it by the usefulness of the reviewed output.

Recipe logic
Why a skillContext mapInputsStepsDecision aidTaste testAuthority

A skill carries the method. The workday still needs current context.

OpenAI describes a skill as a reusable bundle of files with a SKILL.md manifest for codified processes and conventions.1 That makes skills useful for a repeatable operating method: the trigger, output shape, review gate, and stop condition stay put while the current input changes.

A relevant context set helps constrain the response to resources you have selected for the job.2 The practical result is less re-explaining and less irrelevant material in the run. It is not a promise about a fixed token count, speed, or business outcome.

Use ingredients with a job. Keep the rest out.

NIST’s Generative AI Profile calls for defining the supported task, documenting knowledge limits and human oversight, and assessing output against known ground truth with human review.3 The operating card turns those checks into a normal daily habit.

  • Use: current approved meeting notes, current relationship facts, verified project facts, the owner’s defined outcome, and a named source/date for each material claim.
  • Exclude: an entire inbox, full private archive, credentials, irrelevant tools, stale summaries, sensitive material outside the owner’s authorization, and guesses dressed up as facts.
  • Stop: when a material fact cannot be verified, the input lacks authorization, a human judgment is missing, or the requested action is external and unapproved.

The recipe

Choose a single proven job.

Start with one task that has already been tested and that the owner can judge: a decision brief, relationship review, or recurring client update. A broad goal such as “run my day” does not clear this gate.

Write the operating card.

Record the trigger, approved skill, current inputs, expected output, review gate, stop condition, and feedback note. Missing fields are a reason to pause and ask the owner.

Load only the current fact set.

For every input, name its source, date, and role. Separate verified facts from inferences and unknowns before the model builds an output.

Build the evidence and review packet.

Show the card, inputs used, evidence status, proposed output, owner checks, and stop rule. Ask the owner to approve evidence and scope before drafting external-facing language.

Produce a reviewable output after approval.

Create the brief, ranked list, or client-update draft within the approved scope. Flag every unresolved point. Do not send, schedule, purchase, submit, publish, or change an account.

Capture feedback as a proposed update.

Record what the owner kept or changed and why. Add an exact proposed method change only when it recurs. Apply the change only after explicit owner approval.

Synthetic decision aid

The day starts with a decision, not a pile of AI activity.

A synthetic example: Monday’s product-delivery meeting ended. The owner wants one decision brief before the next internal review.

Run only ifEvidence clears the cardOne approved meeting record is available; the current project facts are verified; the owner has named the review check; and the output is one decision brief.
OR
Stop ifA material gap remainsThe record does not identify who owns a decision, a date is uncertain, or the owner asks the agent to send the brief. Flag the gap and return it for human clarification.

Decision measure: The brief earns its place only if the owner can identify what was decided, who owns the next move, and what remains unknown. A clean-looking summary that cannot answer those questions is rework.

Taste test: judge usefulness before the work travels.

Evaluate the task under conditions similar to where it will be used. NIST also cautions against extrapolating broad capability claims from narrow or anecdotal assessments.3 Keep the test small and job-specific.

Evidence traceableEvery material item maps to a current source, or is marked as inference or unknown.
Scope containedThe output answers the stated job without opening unrelated systems or context.
Review concreteThe owner knows what to check before the output is used externally.
Actionable next moveThe brief identifies a decision, a question, or a human next step; it does not manufacture completion.
Feedback specificAn edit becomes a named note with a reason, not an invisible prompt mutation.
Stop rule honoredMissing facts, authority boundaries, and external actions end the run rather than get smoothed over.

Material failure mode: the tool produces polished activity from a broad pile of context. Fix: narrow the input to the named job, label the evidence, and stop when the owner cannot verify a material claim.

Run one controlled test.

Pick one recurring job this week. Build the card, run it on today’s authorized facts, and use the taste test before you keep it. If the card creates more review work than it removes, revise the method or stop using it.
