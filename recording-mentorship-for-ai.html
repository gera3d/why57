Capture the Right Context.

Written by: Gera YereminSeries: Agentic Automation

Useful advice often fails at the handoff. A call ends with good intentions, a messy set of notes, and no clear owner for the next move.

That is not a reason to dump the entire call into an AI. It is a reason to preserve the few parts that matter: the decision, the constraints, the steps, and the questions that still need an answer.

The shift is from a loose recap to capturing execution context that a person can review and use.

The Knowledge-Execution Gap

Generating a recap is different from producing a workflow. The useful output is a small card that says what the work is, what information belongs in it, what the AI may prepare, and where a person must take over.

Browser-capable agents can inspect a page and take permitted steps in some environments. That does not make them a substitute for judgment, consent, or approval—especially when a workflow touches messages, accounts, money, records, or a public commitment.

Core Mindset Shift

Capture only what you are allowed to use.

Before recording or sharing a call, get the right permission, remove sensitive details that do not belong in the task, and decide how long the material should be kept. A transcript is context—not blanket authority.

How to Extract Procedural Value

When you get advice from an expert, ask questions that make the method usable. The goal is not to turn a person into a script. It is to make the decision and its constraints visible.

Instead of: "How should I do outbound?"

Ask: "What is the first decision? What information should I check? What would make you stop? What can be drafted, and what still needs my approval before it is real?"

Those answers create a better starting input for a workflow: useful steps, limits, and unresolved questions—not a pretend-perfect transcript.
