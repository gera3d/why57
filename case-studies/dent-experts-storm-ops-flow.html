What was happening

Storm work moves the same job across field, office, and insurance processes

Technicians, adjusters, office staff, customers, assignments, vehicles, and evidence can all affect the next decision.

What had to change

Move the job forward without rebuilding the story

When this matters

Your field tool captures data, but the office still rebuilds the job

This is the pattern to look for: one job moves through several people and systems, but no record survives the whole trip.

Here’s the Problem

Every handoff risked turning a live job back into a search for context.

The technician had the vehicle, photos, severity, and what actually happened on site. The office had claim status, scheduling, and customer communication. The insurance platform had the carrier’s record. Every time those pieces separated, somebody had to reconstruct the job before making the next decision.

What that looked like day to day

Photos lived apart from assignments. Status moved through messages. Office staff had to ask what happened in the field. The same vehicle or claim context was reinterpreted at dispatch, inspection, submission, scheduling, and sign-off.

Why “use the industry platform” was not enough

Carrier-centered software is designed to maintain the carrier’s claim record. Dent Experts needed a working record organized around its crews, evidence, job ownership, and field-to-office decisions while still accommodating the required insurance process.

The obvious alternatives

Each tool held part of the job. None owned the operator’s whole workflow.

  • Carrier software centered the external claim process.
  • Messaging carried updates but did not create a dependable operating record.
  • Spreadsheets could list status while leaving photos and field evidence elsewhere.
  • A stand-alone inspection app could digitize capture without connecting office decisions.
The real diagnosis

The field app was only one screen in the problem. The actual product was an operator-owned record that stayed useful from inspection through office action.

What We Changed

Put the job—not the software—at the center.

We treated the mobile experience, shared record, outside handoffs, and management view as one product because the job had to survive all of them.

Decision 01

Start where the evidence is created

The iPhone experience centered the job queue, vehicle inspection, photo capture, severity, and relevant claim context where the work occurred.

The practical value: the office received job context, not a loose collection of photos and updates.
Decision 02

Give every role the same working record

Dispatch, inspection, documentation, claim handoff, scheduling, and repair sign-off referenced the same operator-controlled record.

The practical value: each person could move the same job forward instead of rebuilding it.
Decision 03

Keep outside systems at the boundary

The product allowed for insurance-platform and claim-data handoffs while keeping the operator’s job, evidence, assignments, and management view central.

The practical value: the team could participate in the required claim process without giving up its own end-to-end view.
You may be thinking: “Shouldn’t the industry platform already handle this?”

It may handle the carrier’s record perfectly well. The gap appears when your field evidence, assignments, stages, and management decisions still live somewhere else. The custom system handled that operator layer. It was not trying to replace the entire insurance ecosystem.

The practical lesson

A mobile form can capture data. It cannot, by itself, run an operation. The value came from giving the field, office, management, and outside handoffs one job record to work around.

  • Evidence is created in the field and acted on by office or management teams.

  • Technicians need a mobile experience designed for actual field conditions.
  • Delay means continued status chasing and avoidable reconstruction as volume grows.

Bring to the first call: one representative job or claim, the field forms and photos it produces, the people who touch it, and a list of every system it enters before closeout.

Probably not yet

  • No one can define the job states, owners, evidence, and exception paths.
