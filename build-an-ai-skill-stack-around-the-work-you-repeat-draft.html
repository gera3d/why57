AI Executive Cookbook · Recipe 10 · Series capstone

Build an AI skill stack around the work you repeat.

Choose two or three methods that already help. Route each recurring job to the right one. Load only the current context that job needs. Keep the approval with the person who owns the consequence.

This creates a practical routing method. It does not connect every tool or turn a company into an autonomous system.

Where this is useful

Three operating loops that deserve a clear route.

Start with work that recurs, has a reviewable output, and is small enough to test without giving an agent authority it has not earned.

01 · Founder rhythm

Monday operating review

Situation: a founder needs one view of what matters this week before the calendar fills up.

Bounded input
Owner-supplied active priorities, last week’s decisions, known constraints, and a short calendar summary.
Useful output
A one-page decision brief: priorities, risks, proposed next actions, and questions.
Owner’s required check
Confirm priorities, commitments, and what gets deferred.
02 · Sales and marketing

Turn a real signal into a useful angle

Situation: a small team sees a verified account or market signal and needs a grounded next move.

Bounded input
The public or approved signal, audience, current offer, and only proof that is ready to use.
Useful output
Ranked angle options with evidence, uncertainty, and an optional unsent draft after approval.
Owner’s required check
Verify relevance, claims, and whether anyone should receive a draft.
03 · Client delivery

Keep the handoff visible

Situation: a delivery team needs to surface decisions and blockers without rebuilding status from scattered notes.

Bounded input
Approved scope, current work status, named blockers, and current client commitments.
Useful output
A delivery brief with open decisions, handoff checklist, and uncertainty.
Owner’s required check
Confirm scope, promises, and any client-facing language.

Why save this as a skill

Keep the method. Load the current job.

A good prompt can handle one assignment. A skill holds the reusable parts: when the method applies, what minimum context belongs, how the output is formatted, what the owner checks, and when the work stops. OpenAI describes skills as reusable bundles of files with a SKILL.md manifest for codified processes and conventions.1

That saves you from re-explaining a known workflow while keeping this week’s facts separate. The method can be stable. The account signal, client status, current priorities, and approval still have to be current.

The approval boundary stays intact. A skill can prepare a recommendation or a draft. It cannot make a recommendation become a send, a booking, a payment, a publication, a submission, or an access change.
Save in the skill

Reusable method

Routing rule, output format, evidence rule, review questions, exclusions, and stop condition.

Load for this job

Current context

Only today’s authorized facts: current priority, scope, signal, constraints, and owner’s desired decision.

Full-screen copyable prompt

Map the stack before you ask it to run the work.

This prompt starts with the goal, limits inputs to what the owner has authorized, separates evidence from assumptions, and stops for approval before drafting or consequential work.

AI Skill Stack promptCopy, adapt, run one test
# Goal: Build my reviewable AI Skill Stack Map

Help me choose two or three proven AI skills for recurring work I already do. Build a map before drafting external-facing material or doing any consequential work.

Use only the material I paste, upload, or explicitly authorize for this task. Do not request or inspect a full archive, credentials, private inbox, account connection, or background access. Do not send outreach, schedule, publish, pay, submit, change access, or represent me.

First, ask me for:
1. 3-6 recurring jobs I actually perform;
2. the useful output and owner for each job;
3. the 2-3 skills or methods that have worked well enough to reuse;
4. the minimum current inputs each job needs; and
5. what must remain owner-approved.

For each proposed skill, test whether it has a distinct recurring job, a small current context, a reviewable output, and a clear stop condition. Flag overlap, a one-off task, stale or irrelevant context, missing authority, or a connection being mistaken for a strategy.

Create this Skill Stack Map:
| Recurring job | Chosen skill | Minimum current inputs | Expected output | Review gate | Stop condition | Feedback loop |

For every row, state:
- Evidence used: source label, date, and why it belongs.
- Uncertainty: missing fact, assumption, conflict, or risk.
- Authority: what the AI may prepare and what I must decide or do.

Show the map and a short recommendation for the first one-task test. Ask me to approve the map before drafting an external-facing artifact or taking any consequential step. After the test, ask me to mark one rule: keep, change, remove, or add a stop condition.
Use material you own or are authorized to use. The prompt creates a reviewable map, not an autonomous company.

List the recurring jobs first.

Name work that shows up often enough to deserve a reusable method. Start with three to six jobs, then select no more than three for the first map.

Choose skills by proven fit.

Keep a skill only when its method, inputs, and quality bar are distinct. “It can connect to our tools” does not establish a useful job.

Define the smallest current context.

For each row, identify the dated facts that change the answer. A full archive does not become appropriate because the job recurs.

Make the review packet legible.

Require evidence, uncertainty, output, owner checks, and a stop status. NIST’s AI RMF calls for defined human-AI roles, documentation of limits, and documented human oversight processes.3

Run one task, then change one rule.

Use the first result to keep, change, remove, or add one rule. Scale only when a bounded task has earned its place.

Five ways a stack becomes noise.

These are common routing failures. Treat each one as a reason to narrow the task, not a reason to add more tools or context.

Red flag 01One mega-prompt for every job

It hides the real workflow and makes review inconsistent. Split by recurring job.

Red flag 02Irrelevant context piled in “just in case”

It crowds out current facts and makes uncertainty harder to see. Keep a minimum input list.

Red flag 03A recommendation treated as an action

A useful draft still needs a human choice before it represents the business.

Red flag 04Tool connections treated as the strategy

Connections can help access approved facts. They do not pick the job, quality bar, or owner gate.

Red flag 05Scaling before one task works

First prove the row on one bounded task. Then update one rule from owner feedback.

Taste test: did the stack help, or did it add a layer?

Judge the map and its first output on the work itself. A usable skill should make the route, evidence, review, and stop point easier to see.

Right routeDid the selected skill actually match the recurring job?
Right contextWas every input current and relevant, with needless material left out?
Evidence visibleCan the owner see sources, dates, assumptions, conflicts, and uncertainty?
Reviewable outputDid it produce a decision packet, recommendation, or draft that can be judged?
Boundary heldDid it stop before outreach, scheduling, payment, publishing, submission, or access changes?
Stop condition: discard or narrow the workflow if the output depends on unverified claims, stale facts, missing scope, a full archive, credentials, or an owner who cannot review the consequence.
