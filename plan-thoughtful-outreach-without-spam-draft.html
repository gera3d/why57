Where this is useful

Three moments where a relationship map can earn a real decision.

Use case 01

Reconnect with a warm former client.

Situation
You have a current offer and want to see whether one past relationship merits a specific, respectful update.
Bounded input
That client’s approved map row plus 1–3 selected threads that establish the work and any boundary.
Useful output
A decision on whether to share a relevant update, ask one question, wait, or leave it alone.
Owner check
Confirm the relationship, offer, and reason now before any draft exists.
Use case 02

Follow a public business trigger.

Situation
A company announces a launch, expansion, or public change that connects to your current offer.
Bounded input
One owner-selected public link plus the selected relationship evidence for a known contact.
Useful output
A timing suggestion and a narrow proposed move, with any inference clearly marked.
Owner check
Verify the announcement is current and public, then decide whether it justifies contact.
Use case 03

Plan one small event or launch list.

Situation
You are hosting an event or releasing something and need a small, thoughtful invite or update list.
Bounded input
An owner-approved list, a purpose per person, selected relationship facts, and the current event or launch brief.
Useful output
A ranked invite or update plan that preserves “do not contact” rows.
Owner check
Approve each person, message purpose, and timing. The plan does not schedule or send.

First step

Make a plan before you write a message.

This prompt asks for a decision table, surfaces thin evidence, and holds the approval gate before a draft or any consequential work.

# Goal

Turn my small, owner-approved relationship map into a ranked outreach plan for one current goal or offer. The useful output is a reviewable sequence of next moves, not an auto-sending campaign.

Do not draft, create contacts, update a CRM, enrich records, schedule, send, post, change an account, or inspect a wider inbox or contact database.

## Inputs I authorize

I will provide only:
1. One current goal or offer: [paste]
2. One owner-approved relationship map: [paste or link]
3. Selected contact or thread facts for each person considered, with a source label/date and short factual paraphrase: [paste]
4. One clear purpose for each possible contact: [paste]

Use only these inputs. Exclude inbox-wide archives, bulk enrichment, inferred intent or budget, private third-party data, credentials, engagement opens, unrelated messages, contact creation, mass personalization, auto-send, and auto-scheduling. Treat a missing or stale fact as unknown.

For each person, extract only supported relationship evidence, the last substantive contact if available, any current public trigger I selected, explicit boundaries, and uncertainty. Do not invent familiarity, urgency, buying intent, budget, authority, or permission to contact.

Create a ranked table with these exact columns:
rank | person/account | relationship evidence | why now | purpose | suggested move | confidence | reason not to contact | timing suggestion | do-not-contact flag | owner review needed

Rules:
- A public business trigger may explain timing; it does not prove interest.
- Rank by evidence quality, relationship fit, a specific present purpose, and a plausible small next move. Do not rank by guessed value or seniority.
- Use high, medium, or low confidence and state the missing or limiting fact.
- Suggested moves must be reversible and owner-reviewable: verify a fact, ask one specific question, share a relevant update, wait, or do nothing.
- Use the do-not-contact flag for opt-outs, pauses, conflicts, unresolved sensitivity, owner instruction, or no supportable purpose. Preserve those rows.
- A timing suggestion is not a calendar action.

Then provide:
1. A short red-flag list that names any generic “just checking in,” invented familiarity, mass personalization, false urgency, inferred budget/intent, copy-pasted follow-up, pressure tactic, or unsupported claim.
2. A 1–5 taste test for evidence quality, purpose clarity, current relevance, relationship fit, boundary handling, editing burden, and usefulness of no-contact decisions.
3. The one owner decision needed for every row: keep, correct, remove, do not contact, or approve one draft.

Stop there. Ask me which named rows I approve to draft. Do not draft until I explicitly choose a person, channel, verified reason, desired action, current facts, and timing preference. Do not schedule or send without separate explicit owner approval for that exact person, message, channel, and timing. I personally review and take or visibly confirm every consequential action.

The problem

A list of names is not an outreach plan.

Most outreach breaks at the first vague sentence: “Just checking in.” That tells the recipient you had no specific reason to write. It also tells you the plan was not ready.

A relationship map is useful because it holds history. The next layer needs to answer a harder question: what, exactly, would make contact appropriate now?

Give every person a purpose, a dated piece of evidence, a reason to wait, and a visible owner decision.

Ingredient 01

Approved relationship map

The owner decides who is even in scope. The AI does not go looking for more names.

Ingredient 02

Selected contact facts

A narrow thread set or factual notes with dates and evidence pointers. Mark gaps instead of making them sound complete.

Ingredient 03

One current goal or offer

State the value, desired action, and claim limits that make this planning job current.

Ingredient 04

Purpose per contact

Write the reason this person might hear from you now. If there is no clear purpose, that is a decision.

NIST’s Generative AI Profile calls for defined acceptable-use policies, relevant user feedback, documented data suitability, and source verification. Those are governance principles, not evidence that any outreach will work. They support keeping the data, decision, and review boundary visible.2

  • No inbox-wide harvesting. A relationship plan does not need every message you have ever sent or received.
  • No inferred intent. A title, open, public announcement, or job change is not permission, budget, or private interest.
  • No “personalization” by volume. If the same sentence could go to 200 people, it is not a relationship-specific reason to write.
  • No automatic schedule or send. Timing can be a suggestion. Consequential actions require an owner decision.

Build the plan in five controlled moves.

Name one current purpose.

Choose the goal, offer, event, or launch. Then write the purpose for every person before the AI sees the map.

Bring the smallest evidence set.

Supply the approved map and only the selected thread facts that establish relationship context, timing, or a boundary.

Build the ranked decision table.

For each person, show the evidence, why now, suggested move, confidence, timing, reason not to contact, and do-not-contact flag.

Run the red-flag pass.

Strip out generic check-ins, assumed familiarity, false urgency, copy-paste follow-ups, and claims about intent or budget.

Stop for an owner decision.

The owner keeps, corrects, removes, blocks, or approves one named draft. The plan cannot schedule or send.

Practical rule

Do not reward an AI for producing more names. Reward it for making the evidence, uncertainty, and owner decision obvious on every row.

A “do not contact” row is a useful result. It stops a message that would need invented familiarity or urgency to exist.

Run the taste test before the plan becomes work.

Score the plan before it produces a draft. The goal is not more activity. It is less reconstruction, fewer invented reasons to write, and a clearer choice for the owner.

Evidence qualityCan each material claim point to selected relationship evidence and a date?
Purpose clarityCan the owner explain why this person might hear from them now in one sentence?
Current relevanceIs the trigger current, selected, and verified instead of assumed?
Relationship fitDoes the evidence support the proposed level of familiarity and move?
Boundary handlingAre opt-outs, pauses, conflicts, and unresolved sensitivity visible and preserved?
Editing burdenDid the plan make a decision easier, or did the owner have to rewrite the reasoning?
No-contact valueDid it correctly show where no message is the best move?
Stop condition

Rebuild the plan when a row relies on generic language, a guessed relationship, inferred intent, a stale trigger, or a reason the owner cannot personally support. Do not use a draft to paper over a weak plan.

Keep the authority line obvious.

AI may do

  • Read only the approved map, selected facts, and current goal.
  • Extract dated relationship evidence and name uncertainty.
  • Rank reversible next moves, timing suggestions, and reasons not to contact.
  • Prepare one unsent draft only after an explicit owner choice for a named person.

The owner must do

  • Authorize the people, facts, public trigger, and current goal.
  • Verify relationship context, timing, claims, and every no-contact boundary.
  • Approve a named person before a draft, schedule, or send is considered.
  • Edit, schedule, send, create contacts, change accounts, or decide to do nothing.
