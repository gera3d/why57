AI Executive Cookbook · Recipe 7

Turn customer calls into a decision system.

Use the specific call you are allowed to review. Pull out what people actually said, mark what remains uncertain, and give the owner a short brief to check before any follow-up becomes real.

Recipe at a glance

Daily job
Prepare for a customer decision without re-listening to every minute of a call.
Ingredients
One authorized transcript or a small owner-approved set, plus the decision the owner needs to make.
Useful output
A Call Decision Brief and a small evidence map with source markers.
Approval point
The owner approves, corrects, declines, or seeks confirmation for every potential commitment, follow-up, and external record.

A clean summary can hide the exact thing you need to verify.

Where this is useful

Three calls. Three decisions you can actually inspect.

Start with one decision and the smallest authorized input that can support it. The output is deliberately narrow: enough evidence to prepare, not an invented account history.

01

Prepare for a renewal conversation

Situation
A renewal meeting is coming up and the account owner needs the customer’s stated value, risks, and open conditions.
Bounded input
One authorized account-review transcript and the owner’s stated renewal question.
Useful output
A brief separating direct renewal signals, unresolved terms, and preparation questions.
Owner check
Confirm every claimed commitment, risk, and follow-up before it appears in renewal prep.
02

Scope a discovery-call next step

Situation
A prospect call surfaces a problem, but the team needs a focused next-step brief instead of a generic recap.
Bounded input
One authorized discovery transcript, speaker labels, and the owner’s definition of a useful next decision.
Useful output
Stated needs, constraints, objections, and questions that must be answered before scope is drafted.
Owner check
Approve the proposed scope, materials, and any outreach separately.
03

Find recurring objections

Situation
An owner wants to see why a selected set of conversations stalls without turning a handful of calls into market research.
Bounded input
An owner-approved set with its date range, count, source-selection rule, and missing-call limits.
Useful output
A traceable objection map with counts inside that selected set and uncertainty beside each theme.
Owner check
Decide which pattern deserves a test; do not treat frequency alone as customer priority.

Why save this as a skill

Keep the method. Load the call context only when the job needs it.

Every call brings its own account, people, dates, and business context. The method stays stable: distinguish direct statements from inference, attach evidence markers, make uncertainty visible, and stop before any outside record changes. Saving that method means you do not have to rebuild the review boundary each time.

Current job contextWhat belongs only to this review

The owner’s goal, the specific authorized transcript, speaker labels, source markers, and only the account detail needed to interpret the call.

Reusable methodWhat the skill keeps consistent

The evidence lanes, short brief, review queue, red flags, and the rule that a recommendation does not become a CRM record or follow-up by itself.

Practical value: the owner starts with a familiar, reviewable workflow instead of re-explaining the safety line on every call. The skill does not contain calls, contacts, account history, or permission to act.

Full-screen copyable prompt

Build the evidence map before you decide what to do next.

Give the model the narrow goal and authorized material. Its job is to make the review easier, not to make a commitment sound settled.

Goal: Turn the authorized call material below into a reviewable Call Decision Brief for [renewal preparation / scoped discovery next step / recurring objection review].

Authorized inputs: [specific transcript or recording-derived transcript, date, speaker labels, and owner-provided context]. Exclude recordings without authority, credentials, private information unnecessary to this goal, and unrelated participant information.

Build a selective brief, not a full recap. Separate every material item into:
1. Direct statement — speaker, source marker, and concise paraphrase or short necessary quote.
2. Transcript uncertainty — unclear speaker, missing context, inaudible wording, contradiction, or ambiguity.
3. AI inference — explicitly label it as an interpretation, not a fact or participant statement.
4. Owner decision needed — approval, correction, decline, or confirmation.

Do not infer agreement from silence. Do not treat this transcript as a complete record. Do not invent action items, commitments, next meetings, or customer intent. Paraphrase by default; do not copy sensitive quotes unless exact wording changes the owner’s decision.

Return:
- purpose and input boundary;
- confirmed direct statements;
- unresolved questions;
- labeled inferences with the evidence that suggests them;
- a commitment and follow-up review queue; and
- a small evidence map: item | classification | speaker/source marker | concise evidence | uncertainty | owner check.

Stop after the reviewable brief. Ask me to approve, correct, decline, or mark needs-confirmation for every possible commitment, follow-up, and external record before drafting or doing consequential work. Do not update a CRM, create a task, send a recap, schedule anything, or contact anyone.
Paste it into the AI system you already use, then add only the approved source material.

The problem in real life

A useful call brief needs proof next to the conclusion.

A customer says, “We need to see the export work,” and someone turns it into “They approved the next phase.” The transcript may contain neither approval nor a next phase. It may contain a condition, a question, or a pause that needs clarification. That is where a polished recap gets expensive.

NIST’s generative-AI guidance calls out automation bias and confabulation: people can over-trust an output because it reads cleanly, including when it contains a confident error. This recipe puts the evidence marker, uncertainty, and owner decision beside each material conclusion so the review does not depend on the model’s tone. [1] [2]

Choose the evidence. Leave the rest out.

Use

Authorized material with a clear job

Use a supplied transcript or a recording-derived transcript only when the owner can authorize the review. Name the decision to support and retain only source markers needed to check it.

Use

Minimal supporting context

Add a short owner-provided note when a transcript needs basic orientation: call date, role labels, stated goal, and what the owner needs to decide.

Exclude

Private details that do not change the decision

Do not add credentials, payment data, health information, or sensitive facts with no direct connection to the review goal.

Exclude

Unbounded context and automatic work

Do not substitute a whole call archive for selection. Do not create a CRM entry, send a recap, schedule a meeting, or trigger a follow-up from this method.

Call recordings can contain personal information about participants. The UK Information Commissioner’s Office gives a specific example for workplace monitoring: people should be told a call is recorded and why. Use the authority and privacy rules that apply to your organization and jurisdiction; this recipe does not determine legal permission. [3]

The recipe

Set the decision and the stop line.

Write the practical question first: prepare for a renewal, scope one discovery next step, or review a defined objection set. State that the work ends at the owner’s review unless the owner separately authorizes a draft.

Confirm authority and narrow the input.

Identify the specific transcript or recording-derived transcript, who authorized it, its date, and the source markers available. Remove or withhold material outside the goal. If authority is unclear, stop.

Build the evidence lanes.

Classify material statements as direct evidence, transcript uncertainty, AI inference, or owner decision needed. A direct statement needs a speaker and source marker. An inference needs language that makes clear it is a proposal to investigate.

Make the short evidence map.

For each material item, show what it is, how it is classified, the source marker, a concise paraphrase, uncertainty, and the owner’s check. The goal is inspection, not a transcript rewrite.

Hold every consequence at review.

Ask the owner to approve, correct, decline, or mark needs-confirmation for each possible commitment, follow-up, or external record. Draft only after an explicit selection, and keep the draft unsent.

Red flags that should stop the workflow

Pause for owner direction when the output would overstate the evidence or turn review into action.

  • Treating a transcript as a complete record of the account or agreement.
  • Inferring agreement from silence, a pause, or a vague acknowledgement.
  • Inventing action items, commitments, deadlines, or a next meeting.
  • Flattening a conflict, unclear speaker, or missing context into certainty.
  • Copying sensitive quotes when a short paraphrase is enough.
  • Auto-updating a CRM, sending a recap, or triggering follow-up.

Taste test the brief before it represents the call.

Judge the output by whether an owner can verify it quickly. The test is not whether it sounds comprehensive. It is whether it makes a better next decision without smuggling in a commitment.

Check 1

Can every material conclusion be traced?

Each direct statement names a speaker and source marker. A reader can find the point again without trusting the model.

Check 2

Can you see what the call did not settle?

Unresolved questions, conflicting accounts, unclear language, and missing context remain visible. They do not disappear into a smooth recap.

Check 3

Are inferences clearly labeled?

Useful hypotheses are fine when they remain hypotheses. They cannot borrow the authority of a participant’s direct statement.

Stop condition

Would an external action need a guess?

If a commitment, follow-up, or CRM record depends on a guess, mark it needs-confirmation and do no consequential work.

The approval has to be granular.

“Looks right” does not authorize a CRM update or message. The owner approves each commitment, follow-up, and external record individually, then decides whether a separate draft is worth creating.

Start with one bounded review.

Pick one customer conversation with clear authority and one decision you need to make. Build the brief. Keep the evidence that changed the owner’s view. Remove the rest.
